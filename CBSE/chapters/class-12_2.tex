\textbf{Question :} If $x, y, z$ are different and 
$$\mydet {x & x^2 & 1+x^3 \\ y & y^2 & 1+y^3 \\ z & z^2 & 1+z^3 } = 0$$
then using properties of determinants show that $1+xyz=0$

\solution

Let $\Delta = \mydet{ x & x^2 & 1+x^3 \\ y & y^2 & 1+y^3 \\ z & z^2 & 1+z^3}$.

Using the property of determinants that allows splitting a column containing a sum of two terms:
$$\Delta = \mydet{ x & x^2 & 1 \\ y & y^2 & 1 \\ z & z^2 & 1 } + \mydet{ x & x^2 & x^3 \\ y & y^2 & y^3 \\ z & z^2 & z^3 }$$

Taking out $x, y, z$ common from the $1^{\text{st}}, 2^{\text{nd}},$ and $3^{\text{rd}}$ rows respectively in the second determinant:
$$\Delta = \mydet{ x & x^2 & 1 \\ y & y^2 & 1 \\ z & z^2 & 1 } + xyz \mydet{ 1 & x & x^2 \\ 1 & y & y^2 \\ 1 & z & z^2 }$$

In the first determinant, interchange columns to match the second determinant:
$$
\mydet{ x & x^2 & 1 \\ y & y^2 & 1 \\ z & z^2 & 1 } = -\mydet{ x & 1 & x^2 \\ y & 1 & y^2 \\ z & 1 & z^2 } \quad (C_2 \leftrightarrow C_3) \\$$
$$= (-1)^2 \mydet{ 1 & x & x^2 \\ 1 & y & y^2 \\ 1 & z & z^2 } \quad (C_1 \leftrightarrow C_2) \\$$
$$=\mydet{1 & x & x^2 \\ 1 & y & y^2 \\ 1 & z & z^2 }$$

$$\Delta = \mydet{1 & x & x^2 \\ 1 & y & y^2 \\ 1 & z & z^2 } + xyz \mydet{ 1 & x & x^2 \\ 1 & y & y^2 \\ 1 & z & z^2 }$$

$$\Delta = (1 + xyz)\mydet{ 1 & x & x^2 \\ 1 & y & y^2 \\ 1 & z & z^2 }$$

Now, apply row operations to evaluate the determinant:
$$
R_2 \to R_2 - R_1 $$
$$R_3 \to R_3 - R_1
$$

$$\Delta = (1 + xyz) \mydet{ 1 & x & x^2 \\ 0 & y-x & y^2-x^2 \\ 0 & z-x & z^2-x^2 }$$

Take $(y-x)$ common from $R_2$ and $(z-x)$ common from $R_3$:
$$\Delta = (1 + xyz)(y - x)(z - x) \mydet{ 1 & x & x^2 \\ 0 & 1 & y+x \\ 0 & 1 & z+x }$$

$$
\Delta = (1 + xyz)(y - x)(z - x) [1 \cdot (z + x - (y + x))] \\$$
$$= (1 + xyz)(y - x)(z - x)(z - y) \\$$
$$= (1 + xyz)(x - y)(y - z)(z - x)
$$

We are given that $\Delta = 0$:
$$(1 + xyz)(x - y)(y - z)(z - x) = 0$$

Since $x, y, z$ are distinct, $(x - y) \neq 0$, $(y - z) \neq 0$, and $(z - x) \neq 0$.

Therefore:
$$1 + xyz = 0$$

$$P(A | E_2) = \frac{\text{4 kings left}}{\text{51 cards left}} = \frac{4_{C_2}}{51_{C_2}} = \frac{4 \times 3}{51 \times 50} = \frac{12}{2550} = \frac{2}{425}$$
